## Study Priority Section

### Ranked Priority (Highest → Lowest)

**1. Equation of a Straight Line, Graphing & Errors** (Highest priority)  
**Reasoning:** This is the absolute foundational skill required for *every* experiment in the course. The repository devotes an entire 25-slide review presentation to it. The Full Course Summary opens Weeks 1–4 by emphasizing its importance for "simplifying complex equations, creating graphs, and conducting error analysis." It is the only topic with a dedicated "review" file and appears in data analysis sections of Ohm’s Law, capacitors, Boyle’s Law, Young’s modulus, and internal resistance experiments. Mastery here directly improves performance on all other topics.

**2. Ohm's Law, Ohmic and Non-Ohmic Resistors**  
**Reasoning:** Highest raw frequency of any topic ("ohmic" alone appears 131 times). Weeks 2–3 are explicitly centered on it in the official summary. Dedicated experiment PPT plus extensive coverage in the main course document (Study Session 1). Distinguishing ohmic vs non-ohmic behaviour is repeatedly tested via graphs, calculations, and real-life case studies.

**3. Kirchhoff's Laws (KCL and KVL)**  
**Reasoning:** Dedicated full experiment PPTs for both laws (KCL + KVL). Explicitly allocated separate weeks (4 and 5) in the Full Course Summary with detailed "In Summary", bullet points, case studies, and acquired skills. Conservation principles are core to circuit analysis and appear across multiple weekly practical PDFs.

**4. Capacitors: Charging, Discharging & Time Constant**  
**Reasoning:** Strong dedicated coverage (two separate experiment files). Full Course Summary allocates Weeks 6–7 exclusively, with repeated emphasis on exponential behaviour, time constant τ = RC, safety, and applications. Case study on industrial power quality. High practical relevance and direct link to graphing/exponential linearization skills.

**5. Young's Modulus**  
**Reasoning:** High keyword frequency (82 mentions) and a complete dedicated 23-page experiment document. Week 12 in the official summary with engineering applications and case study (aircraft wings). Directly reinforces straight-line graphing (depression vs mass) and stress/strain calculations. Important for material science/engineering questions.

### Repeated Concepts Across Multiple Sources (Cross-Topic Connections)
- **Graphing & Straight-Line Analysis** — Appears in **every major topic** (Ohm’s Law V-I plots, RC exponential curves, Boyle’s Law linearization, Young’s modulus depression graphs, error analysis).
- **Conservation Principles** — KCL (charge) and KVL (energy) echo the emphasis on conservation seen in error analysis (minimising measurement error) and capacitor energy storage.
- **Experimental Verification Workflow** — Consistent pattern across all high-yield topics: Theory → Learning Objectives → Apparatus/Procedure → Data Table → Graph (slope/intercept) → Calculation → % Error → Precautions/Safety.
- **Time Constant (τ) & Exponential Behaviour** — Capacitors link to linearization techniques taught in the Straight Line review (exponential decay linearised via ln).
- **Applications & Case Studies** — Repeated emphasis in Full Course Summary: real-life ohmic/non-ohmic devices, industrial capacitors, aircraft design using Young’s modulus, Boyle’s Law in food packaging/scuba.
